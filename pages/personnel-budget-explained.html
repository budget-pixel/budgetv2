Counting Staff

What is an FTE?

An FTE, or full-time equivalent, is the standard unit Walton County uses to measure staffing — regardless of whether a position is filled by one full-time employee or several part-time employees sharing the hours.

  1. A full-time position counts as 1.0 FTEA standard, budgeted full-time position — typically 40 hours a week — is always counted as one whole FTE.
  2. Part-time hours are converted proportionallyA part-time position is counted as the share of a full-time work week it represents, so its FTE value is always less than 1.0.
  3. FTE measures budgeted positions, not peopleTwo part-time employees splitting one role can add up to the same FTE as a single full-time employee in that role.
  4. FTE totals roll up by department and countywideIndividual position FTEs are summed to get a department total, and department totals roll up to the countywide figure shown on the Personnel Explorer.

A simple example

1 full-time employee, 40 hrs/week1.0 FTE
2 part-time employees, 20 hrs/week each1.0 FTE
1 part-time employee, 20 hrs/week0.5 FTE
1 part-time employee, 24 hrs/week0.6 FTE
What's Included

What makes up "personnel cost"?

Personnel cost is more than salaries. Every budgeted position carries five categories of cost, and the Personnel Explorer totals reflect all five.

1

Salaries & Wages

Regular salaries and other salaries paid for budgeted positions, both full-time and part-time.

2

Overtime & Weekend Pay

Additional pay for hours worked beyond a regular schedule, including weekend and shift coverage.

3

Retirement

The County's contribution to the Florida Retirement System (FRS) on behalf of each employee.

4

Health Insurance

The County's share of medical, dental, and other health plan premiums for covered employees.

5

Other Benefits & Taxes

FICA/Medicare, workers' compensation, and unemployment compensation required for every position.

Year Over Year

What drives personnel cost up or down?

Even with no change in staffing, personnel cost moves every year because of countywide pay and benefit adjustments. These are the largest drivers behind this year's change.

3%Cost-of-Living Adjustment

Board department salaries and wages are adjusted for this year's COLA, applied across every budgeted position.

5%Health Insurance Premium Increase

The estimated increase in health plan premiums the County pays on top of the current Board department budget.

FRSRetirement Rate Changes

The State sets FRS contribution rates each year, which can raise or lower the County's required retirement cost per position — independent of staffing changes.

Common Questions

A few more things worth knowing.

Why do FTE count and personnel cost sometimes move in different directions?

FTE and cost are related but not identical. Cost can rise even with flat staffing because of COLA, benefit rate changes, or step increases. Likewise, FTE can grow while cost per position stays flat if new positions are added at entry-level pay.

Why are Board departments and Constitutional Officers shown separately?

The Board of County Commissioners sets the budget for its own departments, but each Constitutional Officer — the Clerk of Courts, Property Appraiser, Supervisor of Elections, Tax Collector, and Sheriff — adopts and manages their own personnel budget independently. The Personnel Explorer reports a combined countywide total, while cost drivers like COLA apply specifically to Board departments.

What counts as a "Board department" for personnel purposes?

Any department that reports to the County Administrator and whose budget is adopted directly by the Board of County Commissioners, as opposed to an independently elected Constitutional Officer.
