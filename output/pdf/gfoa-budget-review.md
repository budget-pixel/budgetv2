# Walton County FY2027 budget: GFOA review

Reviewed September 8, 2026 against the user's attached **Distinguished Budget Presentation Award - REVISED, updated 8/1/2025**. Budget reviewed: the 133-page `walton-county-fy2027-budget-book-gfoa-ready.pdf` linked by the local website's Download PDF button. A removable checklist is now PDF page 134. References below are PDF page positions, which match the current stamped footers on normal editorial pages.

## Overall assessment

The book addresses all nine content categories with substantial material: a prominent budget message, community priorities, a public-value explanation, program and department profiles, financial schedules, staffing, capital projects, and public participation. The largest weakness is consistency across financial presentations. Several sections also describe information that should be published later rather than supplying the information now.

I would resolve the financial conflicts and complete the capital/reserve disclosures before describing the PDF as ready for award review. This is an editorial completeness and communication review, not an audit of the underlying budget or an official GFOA score. Statements about laws, ballot measures, dates, benchmark statistics, and public participation have not been independently fact-checked. Website, video, campaign, and distribution evidence are outside this PDF review.

The attached criteria permit applicants to choose categories; they do not require every category. They distinguish primary questions from optional, audience-dependent secondary considerations and require **more than 100 points** overall (criteria pp. 5-7). Missing optional media should not be treated as an automatic failure. The checklist maps every primary content question and material-type requirement, grouping the website/video requirements to fit one page. Secondary considerations inform the improvement notes; they are not all mandatory checklist items.

## Corrections and missing detail

### 1. Reconcile financial totals and presentation scopes

- **Revenue, pp. 15, 32, 95-98:** The brief reports $351.5M in direct revenue, while the Revenue Ledger reports $345.2M and includes balance brought forward in Other Sources. Charges for Services are $38.7M in the brief versus $19.99M in the ledger; Intergovernmental Revenues are $29.3M versus $7.30M. These may reflect different scopes, but the book needs a numerical bridge and consistent labels. Explain recurring revenue, one-time receipts, fund balance, transfers, and self-insurance separately.
- **Service costs, pp. 4, 15, 26, 28:** The brief shows $343.4M in service delivery; the message and program presentation show $345.2M with similar exclusion language. State the scope and reconcile the difference.
- **Department costs, pp. 56 and 74:** The overview lists the County Attorney at $1.2M, while the profile shows $1,802,925, including a $650,000 contract. Refresh summary totals and all dependent rollups from a common source.
- **Capital, pp. 37, 39, 114-123:** The portfolio shows $51.3M, while other sections show a $71.3M capital program. Transportation is $35.3M on p. 37 versus $24.55M on p. 118. Sheriff capital is $9.2M on p. 37 versus $7M plus separately identified projects on p. 121. The overlap note helps but does not provide an auditable reconciliation. Show one bridge from tentative appropriations to additional, grant-funded, equipment, and out-year items, without double counting.
- **Capital highlight, p. 120:** The page labels $6M as the largest single project while listing a $10.75M renourishment allocation. Qualify the highlight if it excludes reserved allocations.

These issues affect Value, Revenue, Department, Program, Capital, and Budget in Brief quality. They do not mean all underlying amounts are wrong; the reader cannot currently reconcile them reliably.

### 2. Make the reserve and forecast story internally consistent

- **Reserve target, pp. 23, 36, 111:** Include the actual adopted policy threshold and its denominator, then compare the relevant reserve classification to that threshold. Total General Fund ending balance is not automatically the amount available for unrestricted operating emergencies. Distinguish restricted, committed, assigned, and unassigned balances as applicable.
- **Forecast bridge, pp. 106 and 110:** FY2026 estimated ending fund balance is $440.4M, while FY2027 beginning balance is $431.8M. Explain the approximately $8.6M reset rather than describing the year as simply holding flat. Prior-year ending and subsequent beginning balances also need reconciliation or explicit scope/adjustment notes.
- **Tourist Development forecast, p. 36:** Starting balance $166.5M and FY2031 balance $219.6M are paired with a -1.2% change. The footnote references a different available-resource basis online. Supply both bases and the arithmetic reconciliation in the PDF.
- **Debt, pp. 109 and 111:** Distinguish $29.5M originally issued from remaining principal. The debt schedule lists $8,466,013 remaining principal and $9,102,890 remaining principal plus interest. The wording about $29.5M in outstanding debt is misleading.
- **Long-term obligations, pp. 35-36, 38:** Quantify material recurring costs of added staff, contracts, and new assets and identify how they enter the forecast. A narrative list is useful but leaves affordability only partly demonstrated.

### 3. Complete capital and deferral disclosures

Pages 38-39 explicitly acknowledge unknown full project costs, schedules, operating impacts, and unfunded needs. Those are honest disclosures, but instructions to publish those details later are not evidence that the details have been supplied.

For each major project, add total project cost, prior spending, current appropriation, future funding need, awarded versus pending funding, milestones, completion timing, new/replacement/rehabilitation classification, annual operating and maintenance impact, and the funding source for that impact. Include the radio and bridge-lighting projects among the major-project records or explain why the records cover a different scope than p. 115.

Identify deferred operating and capital needs, estimated amounts or clearly stated uncertainty, reason for deferral, and effect on service or future costs. Pages 122-123 already disclose that the recreation and sidewalk allocations await project selection; retain that distinction. A complete inventory of every unfunded need is a secondary improvement, not a standalone universal requirement in the attached criteria.

### 4. Align revenue restrictions and control labels

Page 32 describes discretionary sales surtax as constrained by voter/statutory structure and eligible uses, while p. 95 labels it unrestricted. Pages 116 and 118 identify source-specific funding. Reconcile these labels using the County's verified source definitions and governing authorizations. This review identifies an internal inconsistency; it does not determine legal eligibility.

Strengthen the $104.5M visitor-paid estimate on p. 95 with methodology, source year, and included revenues. The book already answers who pays in qualitative terms; a detailed resident/visitor incidence study is an optional enhancement.

### 5. Reconcile personnel definitions and cost composition

- **Headcount versus FTE, pp. 4, 34, 101:** Page 34 reports 1,508 full-time positions, seven part-time positions, and 1,515 FTE. Explain the conversion of part-time positions to FTE. Page 4 calls the total and change full-time positions, while the ledger includes fractional FTE changes.
- **Organizational scope, pp. 34 and 101:** County Administration shows -1 on the workforce page but zero in the ledger's broader grouping. Other changes also appear at different organizational levels. Provide a crosswalk so readers can reconcile the +15 total and the Board/constitutional splits.
- **Personnel shares, pp. 34-35:** Salary, health, and retirement shares differ between the two pages. Align denominators and treatment of overtime and other benefits. Label the 47.6% personnel share as a share of the applicable net expenditure base, rather than leaving 'total expenditure budget' ambiguous beside the $512.4M all-funds figure.
- **Hiring estimate, pp. 34-35:** Explain the workforce base used for the 125-130 replacement-hire estimate and the source for the 8.2% turnover benchmark. Neither is traceable from the displayed Board FTE and turnover rate alone.

### 6. Connect program revenues to program costs

Pages 28-31 identify programs, costs, goals, and intended outcomes well. Department profiles add who-pays narratives. Page 29 explains why aggregate revenue is not automatically cost recovery, but does not map revenue to each of the eight major programs. Add a compact program schedule showing direct fees, restricted revenue, general support, and subsidy, with allocation assumptions. Avoid suggesting that fund balance brought forward is current-year fee collection.

### 7. Remediate PDF accessibility and gather external evidence

The PDF has bookmarks, language metadata, and a declared structure tree. However, its section tags contain no content children and its parent tree is empty. That does not provide semantic headings, table structure, figure alternatives, or a content reading order. Remediate the source/export and validate the resulting document with an accessibility checker and assistive-technology review. This structural finding is not a full standards-compliance audit.

The local site's Download PDF link is confirmed, but that alone does not prove that residents can find the published budget from the government's primary website. Retain evidence of the live navigation route. Page 15 is a useful brief, but distribution to residents is not demonstrated by its inclusion in the book. Linked departmental videos are not reviewed here; their existence alone does not establish their message, educational content, audience, or reach. Website accessibility, timely updates, and campaign effectiveness need separate evidence if submitted for those categories.

## Presentation and document status

The book uses consistent green/gold branding, clear section dividers, prominent figures, and extensive departmental detail. Visual overview checks and inspection of selected substantive pages support the graphics/organization assessment; they are not a line-by-line typography audit. Tables and department profiles can be dense.

The PDF remains labeled **Tentative Budget** and describes future adoption steps. 'Final PDF' should not be confused with a legally adopted budget. Update the status and decision record after the relevant action, if this is intended to become the adopted publication.

Some older footer text remains underneath the current page stamps in extracted text. Even where visually covered, it can confuse search and assistive reading; regenerate clean footers when remediating the PDF.
